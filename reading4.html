Reading 4 Reflection

Trash or Recycling? Why Plastic Keeps Us Guessing

Reading “Trash or Recycling? Why Plastic Keeps Us Guessing” honestly made me rethink how confusing recycling actually is. Before this reading, I thought I was doing the right thing most of the time by putting plastic items in the recycling bin, especially if they had the recycling symbol on them. I never really questioned what those symbols meant or whether the plastic would actually be recycled. One part of the article that really stood out to me was how misleading recycling labels can be. Seeing the triangle symbol with a number inside makes it feel like the item is automatically recyclable, but that is not always true. The number usually just tells you what type of plastic it is, not whether your local recycling center can actually process it. The article also explains how responsibility is often placed on individuals instead of companies. We are expected to recycle correctly, while companies continue producing plastic packaging that is difficult to recycle. This made me realize how unfair the system can be, especially when people are trying their best. Another frustrating part was learning how different recycling rules can be depending on where you live. Something recyclable in one place might be trash in another, which makes it hard for people to know what to do and can lead to people giving up on recycling altogether. Overall, this reading helped me understand that recycling alone is not enough to fix plastic pollution. It made me want to be more mindful about what I consume and recognize that real change needs to happen on a larger level, not just through individual effort.
